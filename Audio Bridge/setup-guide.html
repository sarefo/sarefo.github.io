Windows 11 · Android · USB

Watch one video, hear it on three headsets, all in sync

Your laptop plays a video. You listen on your own Bluetooth earphones. One or two people listen on their own earphones, through phones plugged into the laptop by USB. Nobody hears an echo and everybody's lips match. Every step is below — no prior knowledge assumed.

Part A · one time, about 20 minutes

Set up the laptop

Four installs and a folder. You'll type a few commands into PowerShell — to open it, press Win, type powershell, press Enter.

1

Install Python. Go to python.org/downloads and click the big yellow download button. Run the file you downloaded.

On the installer's first screen, tick both boxes at the bottom — "Use admin privileges when installing py.exe" and "Add python.exe to PATH" — then click Install Now.

Check it worked: in PowerShell, type this and press Enter. It should print a version number.

py --version
2

Install the two audio libraries. In the same PowerShell window:

py -m pip install soundcard numpy

It prints a lot of text and ends with "Successfully installed".

3

Install adb — the tool that lets the laptop talk to a phone over USB. Download SDK Platform-Tools for Windows (a ZIP file) and unzip it so the file adb.exe ends up at exactly this path, creating the folders if they don't exist:

C:\Users\<your name>\AppData\Local\Android\Sdk\platform-tools\adb.exe

Watch out

The path has to match. Audio Bridge looks for adb there and nowhere else. AppData is hidden by default — paste the path into the File Explorer address bar to get there.

4

Install a virtual speaker. This is a fake sound device. Sending the video's sound there instead of to the real speakers is what lets Audio Bridge pick it up and hand it out — and it's why the laptop's own speakers stay quiet.

Get VB-CABLE, unzip it, right-click VBCABLE_Setup_x64.exe → Run as administrator → Install Driver, then restart the laptop.

Afterwards a device called CABLE Input appears in your sound settings. That's the one you'll use.

5

Put the Audio Bridge folder somewhere permanent — your Documents folder is fine. Inside it you'll use two files: audiobridge.bat to start everything, and build.bat to put the app on a phone.

Part B · one time per phone, about 5 minutes

Set up each phone

Android hides the USB connection we need behind a deliberately obscure switch. Steps 1 and 2 reveal it.

1

Unlock Developer options. On the phone open Settings → About phone, scroll to the bottom, and tap Build number seven times in a row. Enter your PIN if asked. A message says "You are now a developer!"

On some phones it's Settings → About phone → Software information → Build number.

2

Turn on USB debugging. Go to Settings → System → Developer options and switch on USB debugging. Confirm the warning.

3

Plug the phone into the laptop and unlock its screen. A box appears on the phone: Allow USB debugging? Tick Always allow from this computer, then tap Allow.

Watch out

Two things silently break this. Charge-only cables look identical to data cables and simply never show the box — if nothing appears, try another cable. And on the phone, swipe down and tap the USB notification: the mode must be File transfer or No data transfer, never USB tethering, which hides the phone from the laptop entirely.

4

Install the app. With the phone still plugged in, double-click build.bat in the Audio Bridge folder. A black window does its work and closes. It installs onto every phone that's plugged in, so you can do both at once.

The first run downloads build tools and needs internet. Later runs are quick.

5

Open Audio Bridge on the phone and tap Allow when it asks about notifications. It needs that to keep playing while the screen is off.

Part C · one time

Sort out the Bluetooth headsets

This one catches everybody, and it doesn't look like a Bluetooth problem when it happens.

1

Each headset must be connected to exactly one device. Good headsets (Sony WH-1000XM4 and similar) are "multipoint" — they happily stay connected to your laptop and a phone at the same time. When two of them send audio at once, the headset picks one and drops the other.

You hear this as a few seconds of sound and then silence, or crackling — which looks exactly like a broken app, so people spend a long time debugging the wrong thing.

2

Check each phone. On the phone open Settings → Connected devices. If your laptop's headset is listed as connected there, tap the gear next to it and choose Disconnect. Leave it paired — just not connected.

The result you want: your headset talks only to the laptop; each person's earphones talk only to their own phone.

Part D · every time, about a minute

Start everything

1

Double-click audiobridge.bat. A small control panel window opens. Leave it open — closing it shuts everything down.

2

Send Windows' sound to the virtual speaker. Click the speaker icon in the taskbar (bottom right), click the arrow next to the volume slider to show the output list, and pick CABLE Input.

The laptop goes completely silent now. That's correct — keep going.

3

In the control panel, set the two dropdowns:

  • Capture device → CABLE Input (the same one you just chose in Windows)
  • Laptop monitor → your own Bluetooth earphones

Your earphones come back to life. They're now fed by Audio Bridge instead of by Windows directly — which is the whole trick, because it means your earphones can be delayed to line up with everyone else.

Sanity check

The two dropdowns must be different devices. If you pick the same one twice, the panel says "same device as capture" and refuses, because it would record its own output in a loop.

4

Plug in the phone(s). Within a couple of seconds each one appears in the panel under USB devices with "tunnel on port 44100". Nothing to click.

5

On each phone, open Audio Bridge and tap Connect. The phone shows "playing @ 48 kHz" and the panel lists it under Connected players.

6

Open your video in PotPlayer or VLC. Everyone hears it. Now make it line up.

Watch out

YouTube in a web browser can't be synced — browsers give you no way to shift the sound against the picture. Open the YouTube address in PotPlayer or VLC instead (both accept a pasted URL) and everything below works.

Part E · every time, two minutes

Get everyone in sync

One rule explains the whole procedure: sound can only be held back, never hurried up. So you slow the fast listeners down to match the slowest one, and then hold the picture back to match them all.

1

Round one: make the listeners agree with each other. Play music with a clear beat. Hold your earphones to one ear and a phone's earphones to the other.

You'll hear a slight echo. Whichever side is earlier is the one to delay:

  • Your laptop earphones → the −250 −50 +50 +250 buttons in the control panel
  • A phone's earphones → the same buttons in the app on that phone

Use +250 to find the neighborhood, then +50 to finish. The echo collapses into a single sound when you're there. With two phones, do one pair at a time.

Good to know

Each value is remembered — the phone's on the phone, the laptop's on the laptop — so tomorrow you'll only be nudging, not starting over. Typical numbers land between 100 and 400 ms.

2

Round two: make the picture agree with the sound. Everyone now shares one sound timeline, so a single setting in the video player fixes lips for all of you at once.

The sound arrives late, so the picture has to wait — that means shifting the audio earlier:

  • PotPlayer: Shift + > earlier, Shift + < later, Shift + / resets. 50 ms per press.
  • VLC: j earlier, k later. 50 ms per press.

Play a scene with someone talking and press the "earlier" key until the lips match — usually six to nine presses. If it gets worse right away you're going the wrong way; press the other key four times and carry on.

Part F · when you're done

Put it back to normal

1

Click the taskbar speaker icon and set the output back to your normal speakers or headphones. Sound behaves normally again immediately.

2

Close the control panel window. Everything stops on its own — there's nothing left running in the background.

3

On each phone, tap Disconnect and unplug. Your delay settings stay saved for next time.

Part G

When something's wrong

Nearly everything here has been hit at least once in practice. Find your symptom on the left.

What you seeWhat's actually happeningFix
Phone says it's playing, but you hear nothing The earphones are connected to two devices at once and picked the other one Part C — disconnect that headset from everything except its own device
Sound for a few seconds, then silence Same cause. The headset switched sources mid-stream Part C
build.bat says "no devices/emulators found" The phone is in USB tethering mode, the debugging box was never accepted, or it's a charge-only cable Part B step 3 — check all three, in that order
Panel says "same device as capture" Capture device and laptop monitor are set to the same thing, which would feed back on itself Set the monitor to your real earphones, capture to the virtual speaker
Laptop is silent and nothing brings it back Windows is sending sound to the virtual speaker and no monitor is set to play it Part D step 3, or switch Windows' output back to normal
Phone shows "waiting for PC" The USB link dropped — usually a knocked cable Replug. The tunnel is restored within about two seconds and the phone reconnects itself
Everything was in sync, now it's slipping The headsets and laptop don't share a clock, so they drift apart over a long session Re-nudge with ±50. The panel's "drift skips" counter shows when this has happened
Panel says "server unreachable" The background program isn't running Close the window and double-click audiobridge.bat again

Sound travels over the USB cable as plain uncompressed audio, so there's no quality loss and no Wi-Fi involved. Each phone gets its own copy — a slow phone can't hold up anyone else.

Nothing here reaches the internet: the connection exists only between the laptop and the phones plugged into it.
