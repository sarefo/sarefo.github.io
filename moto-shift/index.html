Manual Shift Trainer

A simple model of a manual motorcycle gearbox and clutch. It shows how the clutch, throttle, and gears work together. It does not simulate balance or steering.

White needle: engine rpm. Cyan mark: the rpm the engine would have if it were locked to the rear wheel in the current gear. Releasing the clutch pulls the needle toward the cyan mark. The bigger the gap, the bigger the lurch.

Where the controls are (typical manual 125)

This is a generic layout of a small manual motorcycle. The flyer does not say which Kawasaki 125 the school uses, so positions and switch details may differ. Check them on the actual bike at your first lesson.

Rider's view (handlebar)

  • 1Clutch lever (left hand). Pull in to disconnect the engine.
  • 2Front brake lever (right hand). The strongest brake.
  • 3Throttle (right grip). Twist toward you to rev.
  • 4Left switch housing: turn signal switch, horn, high/low beam.
  • 5Right switch housing: starter button and engine kill switch (RUN / OFF).
  • 6Instrument panel: speedometer, green N neutral light, turn indicators, high beam light.
  • 7Ignition key (some bikes have it on the tank or near the panel).
  • 8Mirrors. Set them before you ride.

Left side of the bike

  • 1Gear shifter (left foot). Press down for 1st; lift for neutral and the higher gears.
  • 2Rear brake pedal (right foot, on the far side).
  • 3Side stand (left). Fold it up before moving; many bikes cut the engine if it is down in gear.
  • 4Footpeg. Balls of your feet on the pegs.
  • 5Fuel tank. The fuel valve (small dot) is under it on bikes that have one.
  • 6Seat.
  • 7Chain and sprockets (check slack and lubrication).
  • 8Engine and gearbox.

Note: many small 125s have 4 gears (1, N, 2, 3, 4), not 5. This simulator has 5.

The sliders give finer control than the keys. On a phone, use the sliders and buttons.

Drills

  1. Find the bite. Start the engine in neutral, pull the clutch in, shift down into 1st. Slide the clutch slowly toward released until the bike starts to creep. That spot is the friction zone. Hold it, then pull back in.
  2. Move off. In 1st, add about 20% throttle, then release the clutch slowly through the orange zone and the rest of the way.
  3. Stall on purpose. At a standstill in 1st with no throttle, release the clutch quickly. Then repeat with 15 to 20% throttle and a slower release.
  4. Upshift. At about 15 km/h: ease the throttle, pull the clutch in, shift up, release smoothly while rolling the throttle back on. Watch the needle move toward the cyan mark.
  5. Stop. Brake, pull the clutch in before the speed drops below about 5 km/h, shift down to 1st, then half-click to neutral when stopped.
  6. Downshift. From 4th at about 40 km/h: brake, pull the clutch in, shift down twice, and release smoothly. Try releasing early and see the lurch.
  7. Shift without the clutch. Try an upshift with the lever released. Note the message and the shock bar.
